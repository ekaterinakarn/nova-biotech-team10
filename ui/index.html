About rEEGain

We built rEEGain to give people visual feedback while they practise imagined hand movements. EEG drives the hand on screen; the session trace records how the signal changes.

The problem

Imagining a movement happens internally. A person can follow an instruction, but an observer cannot directly see that practice. Our prototype turns an EEG pattern comparison into visible feedback, with future rehabilitation research in mind.

How it works

  1. Record your signal.An EEG cap and amplifier measure electrical activity at the scalp.
  2. Build a personal reference.Movement and rest recordings establish two patterns for the current session.
  3. Imagine a squeeze.Keep your hand relaxed while imagining the sensation of closing it. The software compares short EEG windows with your references.
  4. See the feedback.A movement-like score closes the virtual hand further. A rest-like score opens it. One score controls the grip animation; individual fingers are not decoded.

Who would use this?

A software platform for rehabilitation researchers and, after appropriate validation, rehabilitation teams: guided imagery sessions, visible feedback, and exportable session records alongside compatible EEG equipment.

Users

Research labs and rehabilitation clinics exploring motor-imagery practice.

Business model

Institutional software licensing with setup and training support. Pricing and customer demand still need testing.

The intended value is a more observable practice session and a record researchers can evaluate over time. Whether this improves outcomes or saves clinical time remains an open question.

What we have tested

A working feedback prototype with recorded-EEG evaluation and hardware integration in progress. Our saved 20-participant evaluation has a mean AUC of 0.60 (0.50 is chance-level ranking), with substantial differences between participants.

The score measures pattern similarity. It is not a probability of correct imagery, a pain measurement, or proof of clinical benefit. Eye and muscle activity can affect EEG; signal checks do not remove every artifact.
